Captured entrance tree: "CC0 ガジュマル Malayan Banyan, Ficus microcarpa"
by ffish.asia / floraZia.com. CC0 1.0 Universal, public domain dedication.
Source: https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/038bae6f4bfe4a4f804b63efa2155481
License: https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
Retrieved from TexVerse, Yibo Zhang et al. (2025):
https://huggingface.co/datasets/YiboZhang2001/TexVerse
Database attribution: https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/by/1-0/
Changes: geometry welding/decimation, joined mesh parts, a new UV atlas with
captured-color reprojection for the mobile/software LOD, WebP compression,
scaling and placement. This is a volumetric tree/roots capture, not a
complete rainforest scan. The rejected photograph backdrop is not used.
Large traversable forest trees reuse this complete volumetric capture,
preserving the original connected roots, trunk and crown, UVs and color.
No separate root collar is grafted to another species' trunk. Captured
floor-only triangles below 1.1m in the normalized 12m pose are removed;
the native wood base is embedded 1.3m below the local terrain. The already
loaded capture and texture are shared, with no additional download.
Root Cluster 01 bank/soil slabs are no longer placed as tree feet.

Entrance Ficus close-up grain coordinates (near-banyan-grain.glb): extra
metre-scale UVs and a wood mask only. The existing captured-banyan-low scan's
triangle positions, normals, original atlas coordinates and colliders are
retained; the near view expands indexed faces to carry per-corner UVs. No generated
trunks, filled scan gaps or enlarged/rebaked capture atlas supply near detail.
Original captured color remains outside the close range and on masked soil
and leaves, and on upper branches beyond the near-player wood band. Close
wood uses fine photographic detail from Fever Tree Bark below,
not a newly captured Ficus-specific surface. It shares the existing two-slot
near-wood budget and fade lifecycle with forest stems, only around the entrance.
The photograph's broad color and large-scale normal slope are removed with a
32px periodic Gaussian filter. This supplies fine pores, striations and scars
without substituting the willow's coarse flaking plates for smooth Ficus bark.
The original scan RGB supplies the warm low-frequency color and baked lighting.
Fine detail is calibrated to its measured scene-linear RGB mean;
normal detail uses relative Lambert
response rather than illuminating that baked lighting a second time. Scene
lighting, exposure and the accepted forest material are unchanged.
Authoring: scripts/build-banyan-grain.py uses xatlas (MIT), NumPy, SciPy and
Pillow, never browser/runtime dependencies. xatlas: https://github.com/jpcy/xatlas
Python bindings: https://github.com/mworchel/xatlas-python
GPU delivery retains the 2048px images and full mip chains, encoded offline by
scripts/compress-banyan-grain.py as ETC2 RGB color and ETC2 RGBA8/EAC
octahedral upper-hemisphere normals (X in alpha, Y in green) in KTX1.
Total block storage is 8,388,648 bytes;
no browser transcoder, dynamic code or CSP exception is needed. Compressed
images are vertically flipped offline to match the original photographic UVs.
Color mips are averaged in linear light; normal mips average and renormalize
the original normal vectors before encoding. Unsupported ETC2 devices retain
the original captured banyan without affecting forest near detail.
Authoring-only codecs: etcpak 0.9.15 (MIT), https://github.com/K0lb3/etcpak
and texture2ddecoder 1.0.6 (MIT), https://github.com/K0lb3/texture2ddecoder

  2048px diffuse and matching OpenGL normal, filtered as described above and
  retained as JPEG authoring inputs and GPU-compressed as described above.
  Its measured fiber direction is aligned to each growth chart;
  one tile spans the documented 1.36m. Used only for entrance Ficus microdetail,
  not claimed as a species-specific Ficus reconstruction. License:
  https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
- Bark Willow, photographed by Dimitrios Savva and processed by Dario Barresi:
  https://polyhaven.com/a/bark_willow
  2048px photographed diffuse and OpenGL normal for the limited near-trunk
  meshes below. The scan's 39 existing seam-separated UV charts are rotated
  by branch growth direction and rescaled to one metre in both directions.
  This is generic willow bark,
  not a species-specific reconstruction. LOW retains Lambert lighting;
  no additional roughness or IBL pass is added.
- Island Tree 02 near wood, Rob Tuytel (scanning/processing) and Rico Cilliers
  (cleanup/processing): https://polyhaven.com/a/island_tree_02
  near-forest-trunk.glb preserves the original 27,298 captured wood triangles
  rather than the 348-triangle game LOD. Original leaves and twig meshes are
  NOT restored. Only the two closest eligible tree stems within 12m can use it.
  Only UV charts change; original positions are not replaced by generated
  cylinders. Original atlas photos are not enlarged/rebaked: near clarity
  comes from the separate metre-scale photographic maps. Projected low-LOD attributes provide
  a single-draw geometry/material transition from 6m to 12m. Tree placement,
  original colliders and distant meshes are unchanged. When a closer tree
  requests an occupied detail slot, the outgoing stem finishes fading before
  that slot is reused; incoming detail also fades in. At most two slots exist.
  A fading stem retains its parent near group until that fade reaches zero,
  so crossing the outer forest-LOD boundary cannot interrupt the transition.
  Build script:
  scripts/build-near-trunk.mjs, using the original Poly Haven glTF source.
  Its map-trunk-grain.py preprocessing step needs NumPy and SciPy; these
  are authoring dependencies only, never browser/runtime dependencies.
- Forest Leaves 03 (captured leaf litter, normal, roughness and displacement): https://polyhaven.com/a/forest_leaves_03
  The displacement image is downsampled to an 8x8 low-frequency height field,
  remapped over 32m with 1.1m relief for terrain art direction. This is a
  transformed captured material, not a measured terrain reconstruction.
- Rainforest Trail HDRI (real photographed environment): https://polyhaven.com/a/rainforest_trail

CC0 tropical models and photographic-style diffuse/normal texture atlases by
Nobiax / Yughues, downloaded from the author's OpenGameArt CC0 releases:
- Free Palm TreeZ v3: https://opengameart.org/content/free-palm-treez-v3
- Tropical Shrubs: https://opengameart.org/content/tropical-shrubs
- Tropical Plant 02: https://opengameart.org/content/tropical-plant-02-0
Original palm and shrub archive readme files expressly permit modification
and redistribution for any purpose under CC0 1.0 Universal.
Converted from original OBJ/TGA to glTF with Blender 4.2 LTS; texture atlases
are compressed to WebP. These are sourced textured game-art assets, not
claimed to be complete photogrammetry scans.

Models are adapted for real-time rendering with glTF Transform 4.2.1 and
meshoptimizer 0.24.0: reduced mesh complexity, WebP textures, alpha cutout,
shared geometry and spatially grouped instances. Original vegetation models are not
included; their browser-ready derivative GLB files are redistributed under CC0.
Separate photographic alpha masks are composed into the foliage base-color
textures. Leaf-cluster sizes compensate for lost surface area in canopy LODs;
tree crowns are still sourced mesh geometry, not generated blob primitives.
Two additional tree LODs reduce twig/leaf geometry while retaining the
source's total leaf surface area through per-cluster size compensation.
These are three-dimensional textured meshes, not forest photographs or
billboards. Nearby trees replace the distant canopy LOD with reduced render
meshes, not original-density foliage; the separately limited near-wood mesh
is described above.

Sky-only HDRI: Overcast Soil (Pure Sky), Sergej Majboroda (original) and
Jarod Guest (sky edits), https://polyhaven.com/a/overcast_soil_puresky
CC0: commercial use, modification and local redistribution/embedding are
permitted; attribution is not required. The RGBE HDR depicts
only infinitely distant sky, not a photographic forest background. The
captured Rainforest Trail HDR remains the forest's environment-light probe.
HDRs are linearly downsampled to 512x256 for startup, preserving
captured radiance rather than replacing it with generated lighting.

Sunny sky-only HDRI: Kloofendal 48d Partly Cloudy (Pure Sky), Greg Zaal
(original), Jarod Guest (sky edits),
https://polyhaven.com/a/kloofendal_48d_partly_cloudy_puresky
CC0: https://polyhaven.com/license. Downloaded 1K RGBE is averaged in
linear radiance to 512x256; the captured sun and blue sky are retained.
This replaces the overcast sky in sunny weather only. Its sun direction
is aligned with the scene's directional light. Rain uses Overcast Soil.

The rainforest is an art-directed composition using sourced models,
photographic materials, scanned natural details and real HDR lighting.
It is not a scan of one complete real-world rainforest location, and not
every vegetation model is a photogrammetry scan. Terrain, water, interaction
markers are application-generated geometry. The butterfly uses the separately
credited CC BY model above.

Distant captured-tree LOD is derived from the already credited captured
banyan with Blender Decimate. It retains the capture's UVs and reuses
the original photographic texture; nearby trunks and roots are unchanged.
